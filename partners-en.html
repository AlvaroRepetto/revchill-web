What this is

Revenue management afterworks, with no talks and no stands.

RevChill started from a simple idea: the best insights in this industry don't happen in the plenary room, they happen over a drink afterwards. So we got rid of the room. These are evening meet-ups, free to attend and capped in size, on rooftops and in bars across five cities. No panel, no printed agenda and nobody trying to sell you anything.

Nine meet-ups, five cities and thirteen brands that have already been through them. In 2027 we're turning it into a proper season, and adding a long-form format that doesn't exist anywhere else in this industry: the RevChill Weekend.

What it is

  • Forty professionals on their feet, beer in hand, talking about pricing.
  • Free entry for whoever comes, always.
  • Three brands per meet-up maximum, never two from the same niche.
  • Every piece of material produced that night, yours to use wherever you want.

What it isn't

  • A conference. There's no stage and no programme.
  • A trade fair. No booth and no aisle for people to walk past: your brand sits inside the conversation, not next to it.
  • A channel with mass reach. That one deserves the next page.

If you want reach, there are better places

We'll say it plainly: put one meet-up's budget into LinkedIn advertising and you'll reach far more people than everything RevChill publishes in a quarter. If impressions are your goal this year, close this dossier and spend it there. No hard feelings.

What that budget doesn't buy you anywhere else is two hours on your feet, beer in hand, talking unhurriedly with forty people who are overwhelmingly revenue managers. People who decide, or advise whoever decides, which tool gets bought next year. That conversation doesn't come with impressions. It comes with names.

Who comes

Revenue managers, mostly. Senior and junior at the same table.

The profiles vary —there's hotel management, distribution, consulting, product and students— but the core is clear and consistent: revenue people. Having seniors and juniors in the same room is part of why the conversation works: some ask the question nobody dares ask inside their own company, and others answer without the plenary-room posture.

Why the size matters

Forty is the number at which you can still talk to anyone you want. Past a hundred and fifty, closed huddles form and you end the night having spoken to three people — the same three you already talked to.

At the Weekend we drop to fifteen or twenty on purpose, and there you don't talk to everyone: you live with everyone.

The two formats

One puts you in the conversation. The other puts you in the group.

This is the difference that explains everything else, including the price gap. Both gather the same kind of person, but the relationship you build in each is nothing alike.

RevChill Weekend · New for 2027

Forty-eight hours with fifteen people chosen one by one.

This is the bet of the year and it's a format that doesn't exist in this industry. A house out in nature, one weekend, and between fifteen and twenty people selected from the applicants with one goal: that the group works. A mix of profiles, of property types and of seniority.

The first edition is a pilot, and it happens in Andalusia. If it goes well —and above all if it proves useful to the brands that back it— the intention is to take it to another four or five locations across 2027. Whoever comes into the pilot comes into the edition that decides whether the rest exist.

Why the welcome pack matters

It's the piece that turns a private weekend into content. People photograph what they're wearing and what they take home. A good t-shirt, a wristband, something that doesn't go in the bin: that circulates on social media for weeks, and they post it, not us. And it's what creates the FOMO for the next edition, which is the real asset being built here.

RevChill Weekend · Where the money goes

What sponsorship actually pays for.

Attendees pay a ticket, and for a full weekend with accommodation and meals included it doesn't come close to covering half of what it costs. That gap is, quite literally, what sponsorship covers. Without it the ticket would double, and at that point the group would be selected by wallet rather than by profile — the exact opposite of what we want.

What it's spent on

  • The house. Rented in full for the whole weekend. It's the biggest line and it's fixed: it doesn't drop if fewer people come.
  • The meals. Welcome dinner, two breakfasts, one lunch out coming down from the hike, the Saturday barbecue and whatever gets carried up the hill.
  • The content creator. Spends the whole weekend with the group, expenses covered.
  • The welcome pack. RevChill and brand merchandise, designed not to end up in a drawer.
  • A contingency buffer. For a first edition it should be generous: there's no previous year to go on.

What that actually buys

Keeping the ticket affordable is what lets us pick the fifteen or twenty attendees on merit rather than on who can afford it. It's the piece that makes the group worth being part of, and it's the first thing your brand is paying for.

The second is the content. Without someone documenting the weekend, this is a private getaway nobody hears about. With them, it's the asset that sells the editions that follow — and your brand is in it.

The third is the keepsake: what people wear and photograph for weeks afterwards.

The hike costs nothing beyond snacks. Travel to the house is on each person, attendees and brands alike: it's the one thing nobody covers. The line-by-line budget is something we walk you through in conversation; here we'd rather not fill a page with figures.

RevChill Weekend · What you're buying

Your salesperson stops being a salesperson for two days.

This is the whole reason the format exists, and the reason it costs three times what an afterwork does. At an evening meet-up your team talks to each person for a few minutes and leaves. Here they have breakfast with them, climb a hill with them and have dinner with them two nights running.

What gets built in those forty-eight hours isn't a lead. It's a relationship that isn't entirely professional any more, with people who either are your customers or could be. Anyone who has sold into this industry knows what that's worth and how hard it is to buy.

What you get

  • A place for your representative, everything covered except travel.
  • Presence in the welcome pack: your product, your merchandise or both.
  • Photo report, long-form video and interviews, yours to use.

What we ask of you

  • That your representative comes to join in, not to work the room.
  • That there's no sales presentation. No projector, no pitch, no brochure in the bedroom.
  • That you understand this is a first edition and something will go differently than planned.
  • That you decide early: the welcome pack and the house have to be locked months ahead.

In-kind contributions

It doesn't all have to be money.

We're mainly looking for companies from the hotel sector, usually tech, because that's what the community does. But it isn't a requirement: if you think your product or service fits a gathering of industry professionals, let's talk. Several of the collaborations that worked best weren't a bank transfer.

What we accept

  • Physical product — drinks, samples, anything that can be handed out or put in the welcome pack.
  • Merchandise — t-shirts, wristbands, backpacks. At the Weekend this is worth its weight in gold.
  • The venue — if you have a hotel, a rooftop or a house that fits, that's the most valuable contribution there is.
  • Services — photography, video, transport, catering.

How it's valued

What you contribute is valued at what it saves us, not at your list price. Give us a hundred t-shirts that cost you €600 and they count as €600, because that's what we were going to spend. It's the only way the books still balance at the end of the year.

That's why licences and discount codes don't replace a contribution: they don't pay for dinner or for the photographer. They're very welcome as an extra inside the welcome pack, and as an extra they're genuinely appreciated.

Bespoke

If you can think of a better way to be seen, we're listening.

Everything you've read so far is what we know how to do, not a closed menu. We're a small operation, and that has one concrete advantage: we can put together something that isn't in this document without asking anyone's permission or waiting on anyone's marketing committee.

  • A format that doesn't exist yet — a themed meet-up, a small roundtable, something at a trade fair. If it makes sense for the community, we'll do it.
  • Joint content — a series of posts, a video, a recorded conversation with someone from your team. This is, in fact, where we want to grow.
  • Presence in the material, not just the logo — your brand appearing inside what gets told, rather than stuck in a corner of it.
  • A deal that mixes formats — afterworks and the Weekend in the same agreement, with the numbers worked out to fit.
  • Whatever you come up with — genuinely. Bring it and we'll look at it.
